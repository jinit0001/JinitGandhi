AICines Teardown

I used an AI video platform end to end, then wrote up five gaps worth fixing, each with a clear reason and a recommendation.

Product teardownHands-on testing5 findings2026

Context

AICines is an AI platform for generating video, from short clips to longer AI "movies", with a creator layer on top: leaderboards, Minis and monetisation. Before an interview for a Business Operations role there, I used the product end to end: creating content, browsing the Minis feed, and testing the coin purchase flow. Below are the gaps I found, framed as problems worth solving, not just a bug list.

What I found

1

Silent failure on the coin purchase flow

What happens
The custom coin field accepts $5 to $500. When I entered $1000, the "Get Coins" button simply disabled with no message.

Why it matters
Someone who hits this has no idea why the purchase failed. In a money flow, a silent failure is a direct path to lost revenue.

If I were the PM
Show inline validation as soon as the value goes out of range ("Enter an amount between $5 and $500") instead of quietly disabling the button.

2

No onboarding for the premium video model

What happens
The platform offers what looks like its most advanced model, but nothing explains how it differs, when to use it, or what it costs in credits.

Why it matters
A new user has no reason to pick it. Either it goes unused, or it is used blindly and disappoints.

If I were the PM
Add a short Standard versus Premium comparison (speed, quality, credit cost) at the exact point of choice.

3

The Minis feed needs two drags to scroll

What happens
In Minis, the first swipe does not register. It takes a second drag before the feed moves.

Why it matters
Minis are the engagement loop tied to the leaderboard. Even small scroll friction works against watch time, which the feature depends on.

If I were the PM
Treat it as a top-priority fix, not a cosmetic one, because it sits directly on the core engagement loop.

4

A 15-second cap that does not match the positioning

What happens
Generation is capped at 15 seconds, and a large part of that goes on building the scene, which leaves little time for the action itself.

Why it matters
AICines talks about cinematic, full-length movies. A cap that struggles to deliver one complete beat weakens that promise at the first try.

If I were the PM
Extend the cap for complex prompts, or set clear expectations about what fits in 15 seconds so users prompt accordingly.

5

Free credits run out before real exploration

What happens
My free video credits ran out quickly, which cut my testing short before I could try more complex prompts.

Why it matters
The first minutes decide whether someone comes back. If exploring meaningfully means paying before forming an opinion, activation suffers.

If I were the PM
Look at a separate first-session credit pool for new users, apart from the daily allowance.

TakeawayNone of these is hard to describe, but each sits where it matters: money (purchase flow), retention (Minis scroll), first impressions (credits) and trust in the core promise (video length). The highest-leverage fixes are often not big new features, but less friction in what a user is already trying to do.
